If you need help now: call 911 in an emergency, or the National Sexual Assault Hotline at 800-656-4673, any time. Cornell’s list of 24-hour local resources includes a local hotline.

Where Cornell sends survivors after hours

Cornell’s own service

Cornell’s victim advocates serve “members of the Cornell community,” Monday through Friday, 9 to 5. Cornell’s page says it is “not a crisis service.”

Everyone else, and every night

For round-the-clock help, Cornell lists an off-campus 24-hour hotline as a resource “for students, staff, and faculty.” It is run by an independent local nonprofit. Friends and family who are not themselves Cornell students or employees are outside Cornell’s description of who its advocates serve.

Cornell’s 2026 security report describes that organization as “independent of Cornell.” No Cornell funding of it is published.

Cornell’s own figures

Reports of rape on campus, including residence halls, under federal law were 28 in 2023, 23 in 2024, and 6 in 2025, according to Cornell’s 2026 security report.

In Cornell’s 2025 survey, 35% of undergraduate women reported nonconsensual sexual contact since coming to Cornell, up from 23% in 2023. Only 15% of invited students responded, and Cornell says the results should be read with caution.

What Cornell has announced

On October 13, 2026, Cornell announced added victim advocate support on campus. An outside review led by former U.S. Deputy Attorney General Sally Yates will look at “all related Cornell policies and practices.”

One option

A voluntary gift from Cornell to the off-campus service it refers people to, with the amount and terms set with that organization.

Method

This page uses Cornell’s own published sources. It names no individuals involved in any case.
