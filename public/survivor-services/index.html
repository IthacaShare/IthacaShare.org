Where Cornell sends survivors after hours

In short

Cornell’s own advocates for victims of sexual violence work on weekdays only. At night and on weekends, Cornell sends people to a local nonprofit hotline. Cornell has published no funding for it. This page suggests Cornell give that service money.

Cornell’s own service

Cornell’s victim advocates serve “members of the Cornell community,” Monday through Friday, 9 to 5. Cornell’s page says it is “not a crisis service.”1

After hours, and outside Cornell

For help at any hour, Cornell lists an off-campus 24-hour hotline as a resource “for students, staff, and faculty.”2 An independent local nonprofit runs it. Friends and family who aren’t Cornell students or employees fall outside Cornell’s description of whom its advocates serve.

Cornell’s 2026 security report describes that organization as “independent of Cornell.”3 No Cornell funding of it is published.

Cornell’s own figures

Under federal reporting law, Cornell recorded 28 reported rapes on campus, including residence halls, in 2023; 23 in 2024; and 6 in 2025.3

In Cornell’s 2025 survey, 35% of undergraduate women reported nonconsensual sexual contact since coming to Cornell, up from 23% in 2023. Only 15% of invited students responded, and Cornell says the results should be read with caution.4

What Cornell has announced

On October 13, 2026, Cornell announced added victim advocate support on campus.5 An outside review led by a former U.S. Deputy Attorney General will look at “all related Cornell policies and practices.”5

One option: a voluntary gift from Cornell to the off-campus service it refers people to, with the amount and terms set with that organization.

Method

This page uses Cornell’s own published sources. It names no individuals involved in any case.
