In short

Cornell’s endowment is money it keeps invested. In fiscal 2025 (July 2024 through June 2025) it paid out $439 million. Cornell pays Ithaca’s city, town, and schools about $5.1 million a year. Cornell depends on the city’s wastewater plant, which needs costly repairs. This page suggests Cornell give money for the plant.

One option: a voluntary gift from Cornell, restricted to the wastewater plant’s capital work, on top of the existing agreements.

Cornell can’t operate without this plant

Cornell has its own drinking-water plant but no wastewater plant. Its environmental office says the university “would not be able to operate without” the Ithaca Area Wastewater Treatment Facility. Campus labs, dorms, and dining halls drain there.1

The City of Ithaca, the Town of Ithaca, and the Town of Dryden own the plant. In 2025, local officials planned to invest $100 million in it, and state regulators may double that.2 We found no published figure for the unfunded share.

Common Council scheduled the capital plan for October 20, 2026, discussion for October 28, and the budget vote for November 4.3

Cornell pays about $5.1 million a year

  • City of Ithaca: $4 million a year, adjusted for inflation, through June 2039.4
  • Ithaca City School District: $700,000 in 2025-26, rising to $874,000 by 2030-31.5
  • Town of Ithaca: $425,000 a year through June 2034.6

Together, about $5.1 million a year, counting the city’s base before inflation.

Cornell counts these payments in the more than $30 million a year it says it spends on community priorities, along with TCAT (Tompkins Consolidated Area Transit) bus service, a housing fund, local nonprofits, and campus services such as public safety and snow removal.6

If its property in the city were taxed, Cornell would owe about $15.5 to $32.5 million a year, three to six times what it pays now.13

Questions

Doesn’t Cornell already pay for the sewer?
It paid $2.6 million in water, sewer, and stormwater fees in the year ending June 30, 2023.14 Fees pay for use. They are separate from the plant’s capital costs.
Isn’t most of the endowment restricted?
Donors restrict most of it. Cornell’s 2025 financial report lists $1.8 billion with no donor restrictions.12
Would the money go into the city budget?
No. A gift restricted to the plant would not fund salaries or operating costs, and would add to the existing agreements.

Method

The square compares one year to one year: the endowment’s fiscal 2025 payout and Cornell’s voluntary payments to the City of Ithaca, the Town of Ithaca, and the Ithaca City School District, using the city’s base before inflation. The black square’s area is to scale. Fees, campus services, and Cornell’s other community spending are not in the $5.1 million. Every source has an archived copy.
