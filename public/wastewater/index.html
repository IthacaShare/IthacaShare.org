Questions

Doesn’t Cornell already pay for the sewer?
Cornell reports paying $2.6 million for water, sewer, and stormwater in fiscal 2023.14 The repair bill goes to ratepayers, so a gift from Cornell would lower it for everyone.2
Doesn’t Cornell treat its own wastewater?
Only corrosive lab waste, under its pretreatment permit. Everything else goes to the plant.1
Doesn’t Cornell give more than $5.1 million?
It says it spends more than $30 million a year on community priorities, counting these payments, TCAT buses, housing, nonprofits, and campus services.6 Cornell is one of TCAT’s three equal funding partners, and TCAT staff have said 70% of riders are tied to Cornell.16
Isn’t the endowment restricted?
Mostly. $1.8 billion has no donor restrictions.12
How big is the ask next to Cornell’s money?
The whole $100 million repair is under a quarter of one year’s endowment payout.2,9 The extra $2.9 million a year is about two and a half days of that payout.15 Cornell’s fundraising campaign closed in June at nearly $6.8 billion in gifts and commitments, $1.1 billion of it in the final year.17
What would Cornell owe if its city property were taxed?
About $15.5 to $32.5 million a year.13 Boston asks large nonprofits for 25% of what they’d owe. For Cornell, that would be about $8.25 million a year to the city.16
What else could pay for the repairs?
Higher sewer rates for every user, which is the default.2 A separate, higher sewer rate for large users. Or a long-term payment like Boston’s, 25% of the taxes Cornell is exempt from, about $8.25 million a year to the city.16
Would the money go into the city budget?
No. A gift restricted to the plant can’t fund salaries or operations.

Method

The squares compare one year to one year, using the city’s base payment before inflation and the school district’s $650,000 base before its scheduled increases, so Cornell’s payments this year are somewhat higher than $5.1 million. Every source has an archived copy.
