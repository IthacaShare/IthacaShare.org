Cornell owns all seven. Nearby, houses the fraternities own themselves are taxed in full. The county assesses the seven at $9.8 million; their taxable value is zero.

One option: Cornell pays what the seven houses would owe in city, county, and school taxes, about $320,000 a year, to the city, county, and school district, without waiting for the county’s review.11

Questions

Why these seven?
Cornell’s own chapter list puts recognized fraternities at these addresses.3 The county’s roll lists Cornell as owner of each, classes each as “Inn/lodge,” exempts each as “Education,” and sets each taxable value at zero.1 One is the house where a woman alleges she was sexually assaulted in 2024; its chapter is dormant.5 Cornell acknowledged a lawsuit over 2024 allegations on September 21, 2026.13
Isn’t the county already handling it?
On October 6, 2026, the County Legislature directed the County Attorney to examine whether such houses should qualify. That authorizes a legal review; the houses stay exempt while it runs.4 A voluntary payment needs no ruling.
Why about $320,000?
The seven houses’ assessed value, $9.8 million, at the 2026 city and county rates and the school district’s 2026–27 rate.7,8,9,11 Cornell already pays the city $4 million a year under a 2023 agreement; the wastewater page covers that.12

Method

The seven are every address on Cornell’s chapter list that the county’s 2026 final roll shows as owned by Cornell, classed Inn/lodge, and exempt as educational.1,3 Values are the county’s assessed values, the same on the 2025 and 2026 rolls; the 2026 roll assesses at 95% of full value, so full value is about $10.3 million.1,2 A news count of eight houses and $12.85 million matches the roll if it includes 1 Forest Park Lane, which Cornell lists as a residence hall, and 14 South Avenue, neither on Cornell’s chapter list, and leaves out 124 Triphammer Road.5,14 No individuals named.
