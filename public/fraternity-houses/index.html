Cornell owns all seven. Nearby, houses the fraternities own themselves are taxed in full. The county assesses the seven at $9.8 million; their taxable value is zero.

One option: Cornell pays what the seven houses would owe if taxed, about $320,000 a year, to the city, county, and school district, without waiting for the county’s review.11

In their words

  • “Does not necessarily mean such properties are automatically exempt”

    The County Attorney, on university ownership, to the County Legislature. He cited an earlier court decision involving Cornell and offered arguments for both taxation and continued exemption.4

  • “Your room will be assigned to you by the Fraternity Residence officer”

    Cornell’s housing license for its fraternity residences, 2024–25. The license ends if a resident is “suspended or expelled from sorority/fraternity membership by either the local chapter or national organization.”21

  • “Owned by, and is under the complete control of, the college”

    New York’s tax department, on when a fraternity house can be exempt.10

In court

  • “Private fraternity houses, the same as fraternity houses owned by the respective fraternities”

    A New York court in 1945, holding two Cornell-owned houses on campus taxable. Each chapter chose who lived there.15

  • “A privately organized, self-perpetuating club”

    The Appellate Division in 1965, affirming that Cornell-owned houses leased to fraternities were taxable.16

  • “The right to assign nonmembers to live in the fraternity houses”

    The Appellate Division in 1978, holding the University of Rochester’s nine houses exempt. It called the question “one of usage” and found the houses “indistinguishable” in function from dormitories.17

Questions

Why these seven?
Cornell’s own chapter list puts recognized fraternities at these addresses.3 The county’s roll lists Cornell as owner of each, classes each as “Inn/lodge,” exempts each as “Education,” and sets each taxable value at zero.1 One is the house where a woman alleges she was sexually assaulted in 2024; its chapter is dormant.5 Cornell acknowledged a lawsuit over 2024 allegations on September 21, 2026.13
Isn’t the county already handling it?
On October 6, 2026, the County Legislature directed the County Attorney to examine whether such houses should qualify. That authorizes a legal review; the houses stay exempt while it runs. The County Attorney cited an earlier court decision involving Cornell, and county assessment officials told CNY Central the houses have been exempt for decades, following earlier litigation between Cornell and the city.4,5
Aren’t they basically dorms?
The courts have asked who controls a house and how it is used. Cornell lost in 1945 and 1965, when the chapters chose the occupants.15,16 The University of Rochester won in 1978, when it could assign any student and ran the houses like its other halls.17 Cornell’s current license has the chapter’s officer assign rooms and ends if the chapter expels the resident; when a room opens, the remaining occupants may “select a replacement,” and the University and the officer “reserve the right” to fill it.21 Cornell has used some of these seven as residence halls: 122 McGraw Place from 2011, under a five-year agreement with the chapter’s alumni; 600 University Avenue in 2010–12, with an agreement that the chapter could return; and 118 and 122 McGraw Place until fall 2023, when they “reverted back into fraternity houses.”18,19,20
Why about $320,000?
The seven houses’ assessed value, $9.8 million, at the 2026 city and county rates and the school district’s 2026–27 rate.7,8,9,11 Cornell already pays the city $4 million a year under a 2023 agreement; the wastewater page covers that.12

Method

The seven are every address on Cornell’s chapter list that the county’s 2026 final roll shows as owned by Cornell, classed Inn/lodge, and exempt as educational.1,3 Values are the county’s assessed values, the same on the 2025 and 2026 rolls; the 2026 roll assesses at 95% of full value, so full value is about $10.3 million.1,2 A news count of eight houses and $12.85 million matches the roll if it includes 1 Forest Park Lane, which Cornell lists as a residence hall, and 14 South Avenue, neither on Cornell’s chapter list, and leaves out 124 Triphammer Road.5,14 Legislators discussed whether the assessments reflect the properties’ values.4 No individuals named.
